Notes on the table:
- "peak GPU mem (MB)" is process peak RSS (`VmHWM` from
  `/proc/self/status`) at run end — **not** the GPU's dedicated VRAM
  high-water mark. wgpu does not expose per-process VRAM telemetry on
  most adapters; process RSS is the closest proxy without an
  adapter-specific extension. The relative growth across caps (269 →
  274 MB over a 200x agent count increase) is the load-bearing signal,
  not the absolute number.
- "dispatcher kernel (KB)" is the sum of all WGSL kernel byte sizes
  emitted by the dsl_compiler at build time (captured by
  `build.rs` into `dispatcher_total_bytes.txt`). It's invariant
  across agent_cap because the WGSL is independent of the runtime's
  buffer sizing — it's logged per-tick in the NDJSON so future changes
  to the codegen surface immediately as a delta in the table.

### Findings

- **No ceiling reached in the configured range (1k → 200k).** Every
  cap ran the full 100-tick budget with zero panics. The per-tick
  wall-clock barely scales with agent count — median tick goes from
  157 us at 1k to 824 us at 200k (≈5x for a 200x agent_count
  increase). The chronicle dispatcher's `event_count` is clamped to
  60_000 events/tick (`EventRing` cap is 65_536); past ~15k agents the
  dispatcher's PerEvent cost saturates and further agent_count growth
  only widens the mask + scoring kernels (which are O(agent_cap) on a
  PerAgent dispatch shape — well within typical GPU thread-count
  budgets at 200k).
- **64k tick is FASTER than 16k tick (492 us vs 794 us median).** This
  is artifact of the 60k event_count cap: at 16k the chronicle
  dispatcher walks 60k entries (= 16000*4) and ALSO walks them through
  the consumer arm, while at 64k it walks the same 60k cap so the
  per-event cost is identical, and the mask+scoring's per-agent cost
  is dwarfed by the (now-fixed-size) chronicle pass. The takeaway:
  per-tick wall-clock at this fixture's shape is bottlenecked by the
  chronicle pass, not by per-agent kernels.
- **Process RSS growth is negligible** (5 MB across the entire 200x
  range). Per-agent SoA buffers at 200k × 4 bytes × ~8 columns ≈ 6 MB,
  matches expectation. The fixture uses the engine's shared
  `EventRing` (fixed 65536-slot capacity) regardless of agent_cap, so
  ring memory is constant.
- **Dispatcher kernel size (149.6 KB) is dominated by the fused
  chronicle+consumer kernel** (132.4 KB / 23 bindings — see
  `cargo build` warnings in build.rs). This is the largest single
  WGSL emit in the workspace and a future raise-candidate for codegen
  shrinkage if dispatch latency starts mattering at higher caps.
- **Recommended raise candidates** (next-fixture investigation, NOT
  blocked by this run):
  1. Lift the `event_count` clamp from 60k to (agent_cap*4) — would
     surface true per-event scaling past 16k and let us measure the
     actual chronicle ceiling.
  2. Push the cap higher than 200k (e.g. 400k, 1M) to find the wgpu
     buffer-size limit. At 1M agents and 8 SoA columns × 4 B ≈ 32 MB,
     well under wgpu's typical 256 MB max_buffer_size.
  3. Add wgpu MEMORY_HINTS adapter request + `device.poll()` GPU
     telemetry to capture VRAM high-water rather than relying on
     process RSS.
